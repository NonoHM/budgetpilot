# AISVS 1.0 map of the AI path

For a security reviewer checking a claim about the AI advice, an operator deciding whether to turn
it on, and a contributor closing one of its gaps. To set the AI advice up, see
[AI advice](../ai-insights.md).

Every Level 1 and Level 2 requirement of OWASP AISVS 1.0, with its verdict against BudgetPilot's
AI insights, the evidence, and the issue that closes each gap. It is the AI path's counterpart of
the ASVS 5.0.0 position in [SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md).

**A snapshot of one commit.** Assessed on 2026-10-09 against `main` at `16b3c2c`. A change that
moves a row edits that row in the same pull request; nothing else re-derives a verdict.

## What was assessed

**The AI path, defined by data flow:** every value that reaches the model call in
`src/lib/server/insights/local-llm.ts`, and every consumer of what it returns. That is the
`insights/` modules, the summary builders and `anonymizeMerchant` in `reports/monthly.ts`, the
dashboard route, the two AI switches in the settings route, `DashboardInsights.svelte`, the `LLM_*`
settings, the host allowlist and redirect guard, `scripts/ensure-ollama.mjs`, and the Ollama service
in `docker-compose.ai.yml` and `docker-compose.ai.gpu.yml`. The rule-based insights call no model and
are not assessed.

**The configuration:** `LLM_ENABLED=true` with the Compose overlay the repository ships. With Ollama
on the host or at a remote address, the deployment rows below describe what the operator has to
provide.

**Level 3 requirements are not mapped.**

## Verdicts

Six verdicts, ruled by the owner on 2026-10-09 (#841):

| Verdict   | Meaning                                                                                                                                |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| met       | The code or configuration does what the requirement asks. The evidence names where.                                                    |
| partly    | Part of it holds. The evidence says what holds and what is missing, and the issue closes the rest.                                     |
| unmet     | Nothing does it. The issue closes it.                                                                                                  |
| n/a       | The requirement has nothing to attach to in this product. The reason is a fact about the product.                                      |
| operator  | Outside the repository's control: the model's vendor or the operator's host. The evidence says what the repository provides toward it. |
| exception | The owner ruled that the requirement will not be met. The evidence gives the reason and the compensating control.                      |

« Exception candidate » beside an issue means an exception is proposed and not yet ruled: the row
counts under its verdict until the owner rules.

**Basis**, the strongest one that applies: `attack` (observed on a build by the hostile-model run
below), `test` (a spec pins it, named), `CI` or `hook` (a gate enforces it), `vendor documentation`,
or `reading` (the code was read and nothing executed it).

The AI chain of release 1.3 (R16 on #841) closes every `partly` and `unmet` product row. Its
closing figure is `partly` plus `unmet` equal to 0 in the product total below.

## The hostile-model run

The rows whose basis is `attack` rest on one run on a production build, made on 2026-10-09 by the
method of ANSSI's MEPIA draft (v0.1, 15 July 2026), Scenario 1 and its « AI non-interfering »
component AVA_AINI: the evaluator « considers that an attacker can completely substitute themselves
for the AI module ». A fake Ollama on the allowlisted address recorded every prompt it received and
answered with whatever each scenario chose. Two accounts were seeded through the application's own
forms. Each scenario has a control that shows the observer can see the effect it looks for.

### EU AI Act scope

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, read 2026-10-09 in the Official Journal
(http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj).

- **Not high-risk.** Annex III point 5(b) lists « AI systems intended to be used to evaluate the
  creditworthiness of natural persons or establish their credit score ». The advice is for the
  person whose data it reads and decides nothing about them.
- **A person using it at home is not a deployer.** Article 2(10): the Regulation « does not apply
  to obligations of deployers who are natural persons using AI systems in the course of a purely
  personal non-professional activity ».
- **Article 50 still applies to the software.** Article 2(12) exempts systems « released under free
  and open-source licences, unless they are placed on the market or put into service as high-risk AI
  systems or as an AI system that falls under Article 5 or 50 ». Article 50(1) asks that people
  « are informed that they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious ». Whether a
  card the reader only reads counts as interacting is arguable; the card carries an AI label either
  way. Article 50(2) asks that generated text be « marked in a machine-readable
  format and detectable as artificially generated »; the card has no such marker yet (#969).
  Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026 (Article 113).

This paragraph is the scope statement AGENTS.md asks for, and it changes when the feature does: a
remote provider, a decision about the user, or a use beyond the user's own budget each reopens it.
